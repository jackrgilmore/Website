This is an *example book*. It isn't on the real site; it's here so you can see
what a book looks like: the cover, this "about" section, the table of contents,
each chapter on its own page, and the EPUB download.

Each top-level heading (=* Chapter=) is a chapter. Subheadings stay inside their
chapter.

* The Last Street in Town
Every evening at a quarter past five, Orrin Pell walked the length of Harrow
Lane with a ladder over one shoulder and a tin of matches in his coat. The town
had switched to electric lamps eleven years ago. Nobody had told Orrin, or
rather, everybody had, and he had politely declined to hear it.

"They light themselves now," said the woman at the bakery, the way she said it
every evening.

"So they say," said Orrin, and climbed.

The lamp at the top of the ladder was cold and dark and perfectly fine. He
struck a match anyway, held it to the glass until it went out, and climbed back
down.

** The Ledger
He kept a ledger. Every lamp, every evening, a small tick. Forty-one lamps.
Eleven years. He had never missed one.

* The Lamp That Stayed Dark
On a Tuesday in November, the lamp at the corner of Harrow and Vine did not come
on.

The others flickered to life at a quarter past five, the way they always did,
humming their electric hum. But the corner lamp stayed dark, and Orrin stood
beneath it with his ladder and his matches and a feeling he couldn't name.

He climbed. He struck a match. He held it to the glass.

The lamp came on.

/That's not how it works/, he told himself, and climbed down very slowly.

* What the Town Said
By morning everybody knew. The baker told the postman, who told the school, who
told everybody else.

- Some said it was a loose wire.
- Some said it was Orrin.
- The children said it was obviously magic, and couldn't understand why the
  grown-ups were arguing.

Orrin said nothing at all. He walked Harrow Lane at a quarter past five with his
ladder and his matches, and that evening, for the first time in eleven years, a
small crowd walked with him.
